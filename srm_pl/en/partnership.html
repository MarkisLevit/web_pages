This page is for people who assess an organisation before deciding to work with it. It covers what we are usually asked: who we are formally, what we actually run in Rzeszów, the ways we can work together, and what we do not yet have.

Who this is for

Who we want to talk to

We are a small local organisation — we work best on concrete, countable projects.

Foundations and NGOs

Joint programmes for children, volunteer exchange, shared grant applications. We know the community of families with a migration background in Rzeszów.

Local government, schools, social services

We can take children into free day camps, help you reach Ukrainian-speaking families, and support activities during school holidays.

Companies

Employee volunteering, in-kind donations, funding one specific camp session. We report back on what actually got done.

Churches and parishes

Joint events for children and youth, sharing space, and mutual support in working with families in difficulty.

Ways to work together

Six things we can realistically do together

From one-off help to a standing partnership. We have either run each of these already or are ready to.

A joint project for children

We act as the local partner: we bring the team, the programme and the contact with families. It works well for day camps, children's days and Christmas drives.

Space and equipment

A hall, a pitch, a coach, a PA system. The limit on our programmes is space, not willingness.

Employee volunteering

One team day at a camp or renovating the hall. We provide the induction, a coordinator on site, and a write-up afterwards.

In-kind donations

School supplies, food, sports equipment, art materials. They go directly to children and families, with nobody in between.

Grants and applications

We can be a partner in a grant project — covering the programme side and reporting on the activities we run.

Promotion and patronage

Help reaching families who do not know us yet — particularly Polish-speaking residents of Rzeszów.

Questions

What institutions ask us

Are you a religious organisation?

Yes. Solid Rock Poland is a Christian community and we say so openly. Our children's programmes are nonetheless open: taking part requires no statement of faith, and families know in advance who we are.

Can the Polish 1.5% tax allocation go to you?

No. We do not hold public benefit organisation status, and the foundation is still being registered. If a tax deduction matters to you, write to us — we can suggest how to give through a partner organisation registered in Poland.

What language do you work in?

Services and community gatherings run mainly in Ukrainian — most of our community are families who came here from Ukraine. Contact with institutions, documents and correspondence are handled in Polish, and children's programmes run bilingually.

Can you issue documentation for a donation?

Payment confirmation comes from the platform handling the donation. A full set of Polish foundation documents will be available once registration completes. For in-kind donations we prepare a handover record.

How many children can you take?

It depends on the size of the hall and the number of volunteers for that session. With current resources we safely run groups of several dozen. If a project assumes more, let's establish that at the start — we would rather decline than do it badly.

How do you vet volunteers working with children?

Volunteers always work in teams, never one-to-one with a child, and complete an induction before starting. Requirements under Polish child-protection law are being implemented alongside the foundation's registration, and we are happy to go through the details before any joint project.
